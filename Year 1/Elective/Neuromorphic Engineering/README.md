# 🧠 Neuromorphic Engineering - Exam Guide 

## 📋 Exam Structure 
The exam is divided into two main parts:
* **Oral Exam:** Various questions asked by both professors covering the course syllabus.
* **Project:** A presentation of your group's project, including a live demonstration of how it works.
---
## 💻 Project Part 
* The project can be done individually or in groups of up to three students. The professors provide several project options to choose from, but you are not strictly required to select one of those. Many groups choose to pitch their own proposals, and the professors are very open to discussing and approving custom ideas.

### 🛠️ Structure 
* Generally, the project includes a mandatory component involving LabVIEW (which is covered during the lectures). This typically focuses on converting sensor data into neuromorphic spike trains.

* You are also encouraged to integrate other software. For example, my group successfully used Python for advanced data visualization and handling the machine learning components.
---
## 📌 Final Considerations 
* The course is highly interesting, and the professors are very approachable. One minor issue might be that many lectures focus heavily on their own research papers. However, don't worry—studying from your class notes will be more than enough to succeed.

* Overall, the project is the core focus of the exam. The professors seem to value the practical project work even more than the oral portion. They particularly appreciate a robust LabVIEW implementation, especially regarding data visualization. Incorporating detailed graphs and making the interface as interactive as possible is highly recommended.

The oral exam questions tend to be quite consistent year over year. We have compiled a list of previous questions in the shared documentation file for reference.

It is an engaging course where students generally achieve high grades. I highly recommend taking it, particularly if you are following the Neural Engineering track.
