# 📊 Data Mining & Machine Learning

**PS 📌:** This course is shared with other degree programs. The official name of the Bionics version is *Bioinspired Computational Methods* (the course names can be misleading).

The course is taught by three main professors 👨‍🏫👩‍🏫 and is divided into two parts:

1. **Theory 🧠:** taught by Professor Marcelloni.
2. **Practical part 💻:** taught by two professors, the main one being Professor Pistolesi.

---

## 📝 Exam Format

The course is split across both semesters, but the exam can only be taken in the summer session 📅. It consists of two main components:

1. **Theoretical Exam 🗣️:** A series of questions covering pretty much the entire theory program. You will be asked about formulas, more abstract reasoning, and how to apply the concepts in very simple scenarios.
2. **Project 🛠️:** You must complete a project, ideally in pairs (not mandatory, but highly recommended 🤝). Choose one or more tasks (regression, classification, forecasting, clustering, etc.), pick a dataset, and build a full pipeline. **The project must be approved by the professor before you start** ✅. You will have to submit:
   * the code 🐍
   * a 10-page report 📄
   * a presentation 📈
   * an explanatory video 🎥

**Note 📌:** The project must be submitted *first*, but you can take the theory exam in a later session without any problem. Also, if you fail or reject the oral exam, your project grade is kept 🔒.

---

## 🎯 Tips & Tricks

* **For the theory 📚:** There isn't much to say here: it is a lot of theory, so take the time to understand it properly (it's not something you can just memorize). Personally, I found some topics far from the rest of the master's program and particularly boring or difficult the first time I studied them. **LEARN THE FORMULAS 🧮.** Not all of them, but at least the most important and simplest ones. The professor tends to ask for them and seems to appreciate it when someone gets them right.
* **For the project 📊:** You will need time here as well. My advice is to choose something interesting (your own idea or one from the literature 💡), but don't overdo the task itself. A simple task with a perfect, well-thought-out pipeline is much better than a convoluted project that needed strange workarounds to produce results. Also, check your dataset carefully and choose it wisely: it will save you a lot of time. Again, don't overreach.

> ⚠️ **SUPER IMPORTANT WARNING: DATA LEAKAGE 🚨**
> Be careful: this is something the professor will repeat to exhaustion. Build a correct pipeline and avoid leakage like the plague.

---

## 💭 Final Considerations

* **Part 1 (Theory) 🧠:** The exam is tough and requires a lot of time, both to study the theory and to process it properly.
* **Part 2 (Project) 🚀:** The project professor is *very* picky 🔍. However, the final grades, even if not the highest, are never excessively low (this is our opinion based on our final grades, as the project professor doesn't disclose the project grade).

Overall, this is a demanding exam ⏳. If you rush the project, you risk making careless mistakes. I personally needed a lot of time to do it well, although others managed in much less. The main issue is that the subject is quite far from our usual standards: roughly half of the program is useful for us Bionics students, while the other half could have been skipped 🤷‍♂️.

So the only advice I can give is: take it easy 🧘 and avoid **DATA LEAKAGE** in your project, otherwise you will be heavily penalized 📉.
