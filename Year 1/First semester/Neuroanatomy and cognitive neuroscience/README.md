# 🧠 Neuroanatomy and cognitive neuroscience - Exam guide

## 📋 Exam Format
The exam is split into two mandatory parts:
1. **Written Exam:** 11 questions to be answered in 30 minutes.
2. **Oral Exam:** Generally consists of 2 to 3 questions.

**Note for the 2025/26 Academic Year:** * A **"Pre-appello"** was held at the end of December.
* The course consists of 12 lectures taught by two main professors.
* Lectures were split between two locations: 10 in Pisa and 2 in Lucca.

---

## 💡 Tips & Tricks
* **Prioritize High-Quality Notes:** This is a "notes-heavy" course. I highly recommend recording the lectures and transcribing them. Splitting the workload with a study group makes this much more manageable.
* **Master Brain Anatomy:** The professor places a high emphasis on anatomy. Expect questions on almost anything covered in class—ensure you know your structures well.
* **Written Exam Strategy:** * **Time is tight:** 30 minutes goes fast. Focus on answering every question first before adding depth.
    * **Format over Grammar:** Don't stress about perfect prose. Using bullet points and clear diagrams/schemes is highly effective and encouraged.
    * **Past Papers:** Check the attached PDF; the written questions tend to repeat across different exam sessions (*appelli*).
* **Terminology Matters:** The professor appreciates the use of technical terms, but **be careful**: do not use a term unless you can explain it in detail during the oral phase.

---

## 🏁 Final Considerations
While these thoughts are subjective, they may help you plan your study:

* **The "Pre-appello" Advantage:** If you have the chance, **take the pre-appello.** While the standard exam is fair, the pre-appello is historically the only way to secure a *30 cum laude*. In regular sessions, high grades are common, but the *lode* is (we think) impossible to get.
* **Difficulty & Timing:** This isn't the hardest exam of the first session, but it isn't "easy" either. With good notes and the past questions, two weeks of focused study is usually enough to prepare.
* **The Written vs. Oral Weight:** While a strong written exam is a great start, don't panic if you don't finish every question. Students have received high final grades even with incomplete written papers, provided they perform well in the oral.
