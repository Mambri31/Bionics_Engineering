# 🤖 Robotic programming frameworks and IoT platforms - Exam Guide

## 📊 Exam Structure
The exam is divided into three main modules, each managed by a different professor. You will receive a separate grade for each, and your final mark will be the average of the three.

* **Part 1:** C++ and YARP
* **Part 2:** Python and ROS 2
* **Part 3:** Internet of Things (IoT)

**Logistics:** Usually, the YARP and ROS 2 exams are held on the same day, while the IoT exam is scheduled separately. 
*Note: You may be able to refuse and retake a single module grade, but confirm this with the professors first.*

---

## 🔍 Module Details & Oral Exams

### 🔹 YARP (C++)
* **The Assignment:** Develop a robot controller in a simulated environment. 
* **The Oral:** The professor is less concerned with "perfect" control theory (a simple P-controller is often sufficient) and more focused on your **code implementation**.
* **Key Advice:** Use AI tools cautiously; you must be able to explain every line of your code. Expect questions on C++ and YARP theory directly related to your project.

### 🔹 ROS 2 (Python)
* **The Assignment:** Create a simulation of a robot performing a task (e.g., garbage collection on a grid).
* **The Oral:** This is typically the longest part of the exam. The professor will dive deep into **ROS 2 theory**.
* **Key Advice:** Study the provided PDFs thoroughly—they contain everything you need. Like YARP, ensure you have a total command of your code. Check the comments in my project folder for specific feedback the professor gave on common pitfalls.

### 🔹 Internet of Things (IoT)
* **The Project:** A "bottom-up" project using a **Particle board** (provided at the start of the course). You identify a real-world problem and solve it using the hardware.
* **The Oral:** More of a friendly conversation than a rigid interrogation. There is no heavy theory testing here.
* **Key Advice:** Groups are allowed. While not mandatory, a polished presentation is highly recommended. The professor appreciates students who show genuine interest in their "inventions."

## 🏁 Final Thoughts & Strategy

This was arguably the most interesting course of the semester, especially if you are new to C++ or robotic middlewares, but it's not perfect,there are many flaws. For example the ROS2 part is explained too fast. 

* **Timing:** I recommend working on the assignments during the semester so you can finish them before the winter break. While some students cram during the exam session, starting early reduces stress significantly.
* **The "Oral" Balance:** * For **YARP**, prioritize understanding your program's logic and the underlying theory over making the "perfect" controller. 
    * For **ROS 2**, the program is relatively simple, but the theory is vast. Do not underestimate the oral exam duration.
* **The IoT Experience:** This part is very rewarding and low-stress. Use it as an opportunity to experiment with hardware. The grades for this module are generally very high.
