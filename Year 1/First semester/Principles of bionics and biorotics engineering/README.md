# 🦗 Principles of Bionics and Biorobotics — Exam Guide

## 📋 Exam Format

The exam is split into two mandatory parts:

1. **Project:** In groups of up to 3 students, you must propose a novel bioinspired idea (robot, tool, device, etc.). You'll present it in a **10-slide maximum** slideshow during a **10-minute presentation** in front of the professor.
2. **Oral Exam:** A standard oral exam with the professor, largely based on the course slides.

**Note for the 2025/26 Academic Year:**

- Everything below refers specifically to **Professor Donato Romano**.

---

## 💡 Tips & Tricks

**Project:**
- Follow a **bottom-up approach**: identify a real problem → find a biological inspiration → review the state of the art → formulate your idea.
- You don't need to actually build anything — a well-reasoned formulation is enough.
- The professor is relaxed about this: show genuine effort and curiosity, but don't over-stress it. It's meant to be fun.

**Oral Exam:**
- Questions are generally drawn directly from the course slides, so knowing them well is your main priority.
- Professor Romano particularly enjoys **drawing things** during the exam (e.g., aerofoils, insect wings, locust jumping mechanics, molecular structures), so be ready to discuss and sketch these concepts yourself.
- Past questions are collected in the attached `25_26_questions.pdf` — these are a strong indicator of what to expect.

---

## 🏁 Final Considerations

Overall, this is a manageable exam. The professor is friendly and genuinely doesn't want to make you uncomfortable. Study the slides carefully, have fun with the project, and don't overthink it — preparation and enthusiasm go a long way here.
